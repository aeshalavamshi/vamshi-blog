<div class="section" style="border-top:0">
  <h2>At a glance</h2>
  <ul class="pills">
    <li><b>What it is</b>A new "darshan" (way of seeing reality), arrived at through the meditative method of <em>sadhana–samadhi–sanyama</em>, not drawn from earlier texts or beliefs.</li>
    <li><b>What it claims</b>That the material, the conscious and space are in a fundamental relationship of <em>coexistence</em>, and that humans can understand this and live by it.</li>
    <li><b>What it offers</b>A way to resolve personal, family, social and ecological problems, put forward as an alternative to both materialism and mysticism-centred spiritualism.</li>
    <li><b>How to test it</b>It asks to be studied and verified by reason and by living experience at each step, not accepted on faith.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="section">
  <div class="two">
    <div>
      <h2>The core idea: existence is coexistence</h2>
      <p>In this view, reality is not a pile of unrelated things. Material nature and conscious nature both exist <em>within</em> an all-pervading space, in constant relationship. That relationship is what is meant by <em>sah-astitva</em>, coexistence.</p>
      <p>A human being is the combined form of a conscious self (<em>jeevan</em>) and a material body. Much human suffering, the philosophy says, comes from an incomplete understanding of this: living by assumption, rather than by understanding.</p>

      <h2>One view for the whole of life</h2>
      <p>The philosophy tries to give one connected account of everything a human being deals with: reality and the world, mind and consciousness, values, relationships, purpose and education, money and happiness, justice, and our relationship with nature.</p>
      <p>It describes itself as speaking to four dimensions of life together: the intellectual, the experiential, the behavioural and the material. It says it does not ask for renunciation of the world, nor for indulgence in it.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section">
  <div class="two">
    <div>
      <h2>Problems it sets out to resolve</h2>
      <p>The website frames the philosophy as a response to four kinds of trouble that run into one another: <strong>personal strife</strong>, <strong>interpersonal conflict</strong>, <strong>societal discord</strong> and <strong>ecological crisis</strong>.</p>
      <p>Its answer is a change in <em>understanding</em>, brought about through education, with the aim of an undivided human society and universal systems of education, health, justice, production and exchange.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <img src="{{ '/assets/img/madhyasth/practical-solutions.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Four boxes, Personal Strife, Interpersonal Conflict, Societal Discord and Ecological Crisis, around a central circle labelled Solution" loading="lazy">
      <p class="cap">Diagram: madhyasth.org</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section">
  <h2>The literature</h2>
  <p>The philosophy is set out in a series of 12 books, written originally in Hindi. Four of them are the core "darshans":</p>
  <ul class="cols">
    <li><em>Manav Vyavahar Darshan</em>: human behaviour</li>
    <li><em>Manav Karma Darshan</em>: human action</li>
    <li><em>Manav Abhyas Darshan</em>: human practice</li>
    <li><em>Manav Anubhav Darshan</em>: human realisation</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Alongside them are alternative frameworks for economics (cyclical economics), sociology, psychology and ways of thinking, offered in place of conflict-centred and consumption-centred models. The original Hindi books and translations are free to read in the study app.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
  <div class="two">
    <div>
      <h2>About A. Nagraj</h2>
      <p>Shri A. Nagraj (1920–2016) was born in Hassan, Karnataka, and later lived in Amarkantak, Madhya Pradesh. After years of meditative research he presented Madhyastha Darshan, which he called a proposal for universal good, offered for everyone to evaluate and verify.</p>
