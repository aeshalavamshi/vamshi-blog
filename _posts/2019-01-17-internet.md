## Internet – the subtle addiction caused by ‘reward mechanism’

Narcotics are so strongly addictive that the negative effects are obvious. But what about things that are less addictive and cause more subtle changes?  **— welcome to Internet.** Like any **addictive drugs,** certain ways of using the internet can cause a particularly strong release of dopamine.

> ***Dopamine is a key player in the reward circuit.***  D*opamine is released in **response to receiving a reward or in anticipation of a reward.***  *The purpose of this dopamine is to learn how to get that reward again.*

### Unexpected rewards and curiosity:

*When an unexpected reward comes along, for example: once upon a time, when an early man was strolling along in the forest and saw a water source or a tasty fruit tree, his brain says “hey!!! I didn’t expect this fruit tree!! Awesome!! **what did I do to get that reward? And how can we get it again**?? It releases dopamine and makes him feel good.*

*Also in* this way the brain **initiates learning** when getting an unexpected reward was very useful. Dopamine was linked to the fruit tree (unexpected reward) – while exploring forest was the cue for the reward.

## Internet – a novelty machine, always creating anticipation for reward and strengthening the devil

Depending on how you use it, the internet can also elevate dopamine to unnatural levels. This is because the **internet is a novelty machine,** something dopamine is particularly reactive to.

We are*wired to crave* new information, and new information is interpreted like a reward. If we weren’t curious about new things, we wouldn’t find new sources of water, food or shelter. This novelty is why it’s so easy to watch random news channels about a murder case or find yourself scrolling through social media websites, swiping through apps like imgur or tinder and clicking through YouTube for way longer than you intended. Each of these reward you with some level of novelty for a very **easy behavior** – swipe of a thumb or click of a finger.

YBut… it doesn’t end. You can still swipe for the chance to get another cool picture so your dopamine remains elevated. **This never ending novelty is what leads to the abnormal elevation of dopamine.**

## Explaining compulsive/ addictive behaviours

Dopamine keeps track of what behaviors done in what situations will get you rewards, and then motivates you to do those behaviors. Browsing forest and exploring forest was the **cue** and the r**eward was finding** ***‘something!! something useful and a dopamine release!!*****.**

This explains why ***cues like seeing a bar where we drink alcohol or seeing a friend smoking cigarette can trigger dopamine rises and therefore cravings in us.*** It needs some helping hand from prefrontal cortex’s job is to get you to stop. Our angel must be strong.

## Why is this novelty machine all the more dangerous?

1. **Easy rewards making addiction very easy**
2. **Homeostasis**
3. Design of internet products is done in a such a way to keep you **hooked**

### 2. It is easy in the start until it hits a slippery slope – Homeostasis

#### Why it is harder to get out after it is too late?

Our bodies are constantly trying to remain in a state of balance- this is called “**homeostasis**”. Things like your blood sugar levels, Ph level, your temperature and blood pressure are all finely regulated.

Stimulation is also something your body tries to regulate. For example, heavy internet users constantly activate dopamine receptors. *They browse their feed hoping they find something interesting*. *Something useful*. **They are able to ignore 10 useless feeds, to hit the interesting 11th feed**. And once they hit, there is a dopamine rise. A small rise is enough to keep us ‘hooked’.

> *To maintain balance and regulate stimulation, the brain “downregulates” or decreases the number of receptors available and the user becomes less responsible.*

Less receptors available means less dopamine signaling and it becomes ***harder for everyday activities to provide enough dopamine to motivate the heavy internet user.***

***Because of this, they will start to lose interest in hobbies and long term goals which require much more effort and don’t provide as much dopamine.*** Receptor downregulation decreases general wanting and motivation to do everyday things. However, craving or wanting for the internet, drastically increases.

> Now imagine the ways **your and your children’s brains** are altering!!

I personally have participated in meeting for designing apps. The only agenda is how to keep people ‘Coming back’ to the platform? How to make visitors spend more time?. This appears very unethical, but apps are designed to keep you ‘hooked’.

(review will be done in future) that the key to a successful content platform is having the cue to use the website or application come from within the user.

> *A negative feeling is most effective – being bored might be a trigger to use Reddit/ YouTube and being lonely would be a trigger to use Tinder or Facebook. This is very powerful because the cue can come at any time and lies ‘within’ the user. Apps should be designed with internal triggers in mind.*
